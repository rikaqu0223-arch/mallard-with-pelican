# The Mallard and the Pelican

A mallard and a pelican lived beside a wide lake.

Each morning, the mallard searched for food among the reeds. The pelican caught fish in deeper water.

“My bill can hold more than you can eat,” said the pelican.

“My feet can take me where you cannot go,” said the mallard.

Each thought his gift was better.

One summer, the lake began to dry. Fish gathered in a small pool behind a bank of mud. The pelican saw them from the air, but the reeds were too thick for him to reach the water.

The mallard found a narrow path into the pool. He could enter, but he could not carry the fish to the lake.

“Your large bill is of no use here,” he called.

“Nor are your small feet,” the pelican replied.

They watched the water sink.

Then the mallard stopped arguing.

“I can guide the fish through the reeds,” he said. “Can you carry them?”

The pelican lowered his bill at the end of the path.

All afternoon, the mallard drove fish toward him. The pelican carried them to the lake. By sunset, the pool was empty.

“Your feet saved them,” said the pelican.

“Your bill saved them,” said the mallard.

After that, they still compared their catches. But they no longer compared their worth.

**Moral:** Different gifts are most useful when used together.
